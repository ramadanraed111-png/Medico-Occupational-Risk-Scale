# IOFI Job & Task Library – Logistics, Transportation, Security & Emergency Response

**Document ID:** IOFI-JL-07-08  
**Status:** Working library draft – for technical review  
**Version:** 0.1 | **Prepared:** 14 November 2026  
**Scope:** Petroleum and natural-gas production, drilling support, processing sites, remote/onshore facilities and offshore logistics where applicable.

> This document is a structured job/task evidence library, not a medical standard, legal determination, exposure survey, fitness certificate or approved risk score. A task is included as a plausible profile only. Site-specific applicability, measurements, controls and worker duties must be verified before use.

## 1. Library rules and evidence fields

Each IOFI task record should ultimately carry: family/job/task identifiers; task description; site and operating mode; worker group/contractor; exposure route; health hazard; operational safety hazard (separate field); exposure frequency and duration (evidence-based); measured value, unit, sampling method and averaging period where relevant; qualitative estimate and rationale where measurement is unavailable; controls and evidence of effectiveness; essential physical, sensory, cognitive and psychosocial demands; emergency/criticality requirements; source, version, date and confidence; review owner and review date.

Do not infer exposure merely from a job title. Confirm the actual task, work location, process, time spent, work pattern, equipment, nearby sources and controls. Mark unavailable evidence as **Unknown / Pending verification**, never as zero. Record personal exposure and area measurements distinctly. Record peak, ceiling, STEL, TWA or dose only where appropriate to the substance or agent and applicable method.

### Separation of evidence domains

- **Health exposure (OERI):** chemical, physical, biological, ergonomic and psychosocial agents with exposure routes and dose/context evidence.
- **Operational safety (TRA / Critical Safety Gate):** vehicle collision, struck-by/caught-between, lifting, dropped objects, fire/explosion, electrical energy, work at height, confined-space entry, line-of-fire and rescue hazards. These are not converted into health-exposure doses.
- **Health vulnerability (IHVI):** physician-restricted consideration of a person's health stability and vulnerability in relation to a verified exposure or essential task. Diagnosis alone is not a score or disqualifier.
- **Job/task compatibility (JTCI):** actual functional capability against essential task demands, including safe driving, load handling, communication, emergency duties and use of required equipment.
- **Worksite/operational modifier (WORM):** site remoteness, access, climate, communications, medical capability, evacuation time and rescue resources. It modifies task context and controls, not a worker's worth or a medical score.

### 2.3 Operational hazards – keep separate from health exposure

- Road traffic collision, rollover, loss of control, reversing and pedestrian interface.
- Fatigue-related driving, distraction, speeding, unsafe journey planning and adverse road/weather conditions.
- Vehicle defects, tyre/brake failure, poor road surface, poor lighting and inadequate seat-belt use.
- Load shift, dropped loads, unstable stacking, crush/pinch points and line-of-fire exposure.
- Forklift overturn, pedestrian strike, load fall and operation in unsuitable ventilation conditions.
- Crane instability, overload, suspended load, lifting accessory failure, poor ground bearing and communication failure.
- Loading/unloading near flammable atmospheres, ignition sources or moving process equipment.
- Marine transfer hazards: vessel movement, dropped cargo, suspended loads, slips/trips and man-overboard.

### 2.4 Minimum control evidence to capture

1. **Journey management:** business-need review, route selection, weather/road assessment, journey plan, check-in/escalation, journey closure and contingency arrangements.
2. **Driver fitness for the journey:** verified authorization/licence, competency, pre-journey fatigue/self-check, rest opportunity, working/driving-hour controls and a clear stop-work process. Do not rely on a self-declaration alone where other evidence indicates fatigue.
3. **Vehicle controls:** preventive maintenance, defect reporting, seat belts, suitable vehicle selection, restraints, speed management, reversing aids, IVMS/telematics where used and documented review of trends.
4. **Load controls:** weight/dimension verification, load distribution, rated securing equipment, inspection, segregation, compatible storage, exclusion zones and competent lifting teams.
5. **Powered industrial trucks:** competent authorized operators, equipment inspection, speed/route controls, pedestrian segregation, load capacity and suitable ventilation for combustion-powered equipment.
6. **Lifting operations:** lift plan, ground assessment, equipment/accessory certification and inspection, competent operator/riggers/banksman, communication, exclusion zone and weather limits.
7. **Chemical/fuel interface:** current SDS, product identification, closed transfer where practicable, compatible connections, spill response, bonding/earthing where required, ventilation, gas testing where justified, eyewash/shower and trained responders.
8. **Ergonomics:** mechanical aids, load limits derived from task assessment, team handling where suitable, layout redesign, adjustable workstations and recovery breaks.
9. **Evidence quality:** identify the control owner, date, inspection record, maintenance status, training record and whether the control was observed working.

### 2.5 Job demand profiles – essential requirements, not generic pass/fail tests

| Role | Physical / sensory | Cognitive / psychosocial | Critical task conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light / heavy vehicle driver | Safe vehicle entry/exit; sustained seated tolerance; adequate functional vision and hearing for driving; limb coordination | Sustained attention, hazard anticipation, judgement, route planning, fatigue awareness | Safe driving under actual route, vehicle, shift and weather conditions; applicable legal licensing requirements |
| Crew bus driver | Driver requirements plus passenger communication and evacuation movement as assigned | Calm communication, passenger management, emergency decision-making | Ability to stop safely, communicate and follow evacuation plan |
| Forklift operator | Safe mounting/dismounting; visual field and depth perception; control coordination; hearing/visual alarm awareness | Spatial judgement, attention, rule compliance | Controlled movement and load placement in actual site layout |
| Crane operator | Sustained posture; visual/signal perception; control coordination | Spatial judgement, concentration, communication under pressure | Follow lift plan and stop when visibility, communications or conditions are unsafe |
| Warehouse / loading operative | Mobility, grip, postural tolerance; task-specific manual handling capacity | Label recognition, inventory accuracy, hazard awareness | Use mechanical aids and stay outside suspended-load/vehicle zones |
| Fleet coordinator / dispatcher | Screen/desk tolerance; hearing and speech communication | Multitasking, prioritization, clear escalation, shift-work tolerance | Accurate journey tracking and emergency escalation |

No universal weight, distance, duration, visual-acuity cut-off or driving-hour threshold is specified here. Such criteria require applicable law, task analysis, equipment design, medical/occupational review and documented company policy.

### 3.3 Operational hazards – keep separate from health exposure

- Armed threat, violence, aggression, unauthorized access and security incidents.
- Vehicle collision during patrol, reversing and pedestrian interaction.
- Fire growth, flash fire, explosion, structural collapse, falling objects and secondary releases.
- Uncontrolled H2S/toxic-gas release, oxygen-deficient atmosphere and flammable atmosphere.
- Confined-space entrapment, engulfment, fall, dropped objects and failed retrieval systems.
- Thermal injury, burns, heat exhaustion and loss of orientation during firefighting/rescue.
- Electrical hazards, hazardous energy, unstable structures, pressurized systems and moving equipment.
- Rescue-team exposure due to unplanned entry, inadequate monitoring, poor communications or insufficient backup.
- Casualty handling injuries, contamination spread and unsafe decontamination.
- Fatigue, stress, traumatic incident exposure and impaired decision-making. Psychosocial hazards require prevention/support systems and must not be used to stigmatize responders.

### 3.4 Minimum control evidence to capture

1. **Security work:** post orders, access protocols, communication/radio checks, patrol route and check-in, lone-worker controls, escalation and de-escalation training, safe transport and incident support.
2. **Fire response:** incident command, pre-incident plans, water/foam capability, equipment inspection, SCBA maintenance and fit testing, team accountability, backup/rescue arrangements, rehab/rest and post-incident medical follow-up as appropriate.
3. **Atmospheric hazards:** calibrated direct-reading monitors, appropriate sampling strategy, oxygen/flammability/toxic-gas assessment, continuous monitoring where required, alarm/action thresholds sourced from the governing procedure and applicable limits.
4. **H2S / IDLH response:** no unprotected entry; trained and equipped responders; positive-pressure SCBA appropriate to the hazard; standby/rescue capability; communications and retrieval arrangements. Odour must never be used as a detector.
5. **Confined-space rescue:** permit and isolation verification, atmospheric testing from outside, ventilation where appropriate, attendant, communication, retrieval system, rescue plan and competent team. Do not improvise entry rescue.
6. **HazMat / spill:** product identification and SDS, incident command, exclusion/hot-warm-cold zones as appropriate, monitoring, PPE selected for the substance and task, decontamination and waste handling.
7. **Medical response:** infection-prevention procedures, sharps safety, appropriate PPE, safe patient handling, clinical scope/credential verification and clear handover.
8. **Heat management:** work/rest and acclimatization arrangements based on measured conditions and applicable guidance, hydration, cooling/rehabilitation area, buddy monitoring and stop-work triggers.
9. **Psychosocial support:** adequate staffing, predictable rotations where practicable, recovery time, post-incident support, confidential referral pathways and non-punitive reporting.
10. **Drills:** realistic but controlled scenarios, documented objectives, competency observation, stop criteria, after-action review and corrective-action closure.

### 3.5 Job demand profiles – essential requirements, not generic pass/fail tests

| Role | Physical / sensory | Cognitive / psychosocial | Critical task conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security guard / patrol | Standing/walking on actual terrain; functional hearing/vision for observation; safe mobility | Vigilance, communication, conflict de-escalation, judgement | Ability to follow post orders, summon support and avoid unsafe confrontation |
| Control room operator | Sustained screen and radio use; visual/auditory signal discrimination | Sustained attention, alarm prioritization, communication under pressure | Reliable alarm recognition and escalation; fatigue risk management for shifts |
| Firefighter | Task-specific mobility, strength/endurance, heat tolerance and ability to use assigned PPE/SCBA | Team coordination, orientation, rapid judgement, adherence to command | Medical and functional evaluation must be linked to actual firefighting duties and equipment; no diagnosis-only exclusion |
| Rescue technician | Mobility, balance, strength/endurance, dexterity, ability to use harness/respiratory equipment | Spatial reasoning, teamwork, communication, controlled decision-making | Competence and physical capacity for the defined rescue method, environment and backup system |
| HazMat responder | Ability to wear and work in selected protective ensemble; mobility and dexterity | Hazard recognition, procedural discipline, monitoring interpretation | Task- and chemical-specific PPE, training and medical surveillance where required |
| Emergency medical responder | Mobility for casualty access/handling; dexterity and communication | Clinical judgement within scope, composure, prioritization | Safe care, infection control, team communication and handover |
| Emergency coordinator | Communication and mobility appropriate to command post/field role | Situational awareness, prioritization, coordination and decision-making | Clear command structure, reliable communications and authority to stop unsafe response |

No universal aerobic fitness score, lifting test, BMI, diagnosis list or clinical cut-off is specified. Any physical/medical standard for emergency responders must be evidence-based, job-related, validated for the actual role, consistently applied and reviewed by occupational-health and operational experts.

## 4. WORM site profile – fields to collect for both families

- Worksite type: onshore / offshore / marine / remote / urban / processing / drilling / warehouse.
- Access: road condition, journey duration, seasonal constraints, night travel, transport availability.
- Communications: radio/mobile/satellite coverage, dead zones, backup channels and escalation process.
- Medical capability: first aid, clinic/medic, physician access, ambulance capability, receiving facility and referral route.
- Emergency response: fire/rescue team capability, equipment, mutual aid, external response availability and verified response time.
- Evacuation: routes, muster points, transport, weather/sea-state limitations and medical evacuation arrangements.
- Environmental context: heat, humidity, wind, dust, lightning, visibility and other site-relevant conditions.
- Staffing: shift pattern, lone work, contractor interfaces, minimum team composition and relief coverage.
- Evidence: source, owner, date last verified, operational status and contingency if unavailable.

Do not infer response time or medical capability from a site label. Verify actual resources and run exercises.

## 6. Quality and governance checks before operational use

- Validate every job/task against current job descriptions, field observation, worker/contractor consultation and site-specific JSA/JHA.
- Confirm applicable Egyptian legal requirements and company standards with competent local HSE/legal reviewers. International OSHA/NIOSH material below is technical reference, not automatically Egyptian law.
- Maintain a jurisdictional exposure-limit register: Egyptian legal limit; company limit; external guideline/standard; source, edition, date, substance/agent, averaging period and applicability. Never silently substitute a foreign limit for an Egyptian legal limit.
- Require competent occupational hygienist review for sampling strategy and interpretation.
- Require physician review for confidential health-vulnerability and fitness-related interpretation.
- Provide HSE only with the functional restrictions and operational controls needed for safe work; do not expose diagnoses or confidential clinical notes.
- Preserve version history, change rationale, reviewer, approval status and effective date.
- Treat this draft as a source library only. It does not contain validated weights, numerical cut-offs or automatic fitness outcomes.

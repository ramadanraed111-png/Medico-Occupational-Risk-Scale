# IOFI Job & Task Library – Families 01–06 and 09–10

**Document ID:** IOFI-JL-01-06-09-10  
**Version:** 0.1 | **Prepared:** 14 November 2026  
**Status:** Structured working draft for site, occupational-health and HSE review  
**Scope:** Petroleum and natural-gas drilling, well services, production, processing, maintenance, HSE, occupational health, laboratories, quality, specialist contractors and support operations.

> This is a task/evidence library, not an exposure survey, legal determination, clinical standard, risk score or fitness decision. Job titles are not proof of exposure. Every task, agent, route, intensity, duration, control and essential demand must be confirmed against the actual work, process, location, roster and worker assignment.

## 1. Mandatory data and interpretation rules

For each task record, capture: family/job/task IDs; task description and operating mode; site/unit; employee or contractor group; exposure agent and route; measurement or qualitative rationale; unit, method, sampling duration and averaging period where relevant; frequency and duration; controls and verification evidence; operational safety hazards in a separate field; essential physical, sensory, cognitive and psychosocial demands; emergency duties; source/version/date; confidence; reviewer and review date.

- Use **Unknown / Pending verification** for missing evidence. Never convert missing data to zero or “no exposure”.
- Separate personal exposure measurements from area monitoring. Preserve agent-specific units and distinguish peak, ceiling, STEL, TWA or dose where the method requires it.
- A potential agent is a prompt to investigate, not an assertion that exposure exists.
- Do not merge health exposure (OERI), operational safety (TRA / Critical Safety Gate), physician-led health vulnerability (IHVI), functional task compatibility (JTCI) and site context (WORM).
- Do not apply numerical weights, universal exposure cut-offs, compensatory averaging or automated fitness outcomes in this library.
- A critical task requirement must be reviewed task-by-task; strengths in unrelated domains cannot compensate for inability to perform a genuinely essential safety-critical task.
- Distinguish routine, non-routine, shutdown/turnaround, startup, upset, maintenance and emergency modes.

## 10. Cross-family job-demand profile fields (JTCI)

For each task, describe rather than grade the requirement:
- **Physical:** walking/standing, climbing, balance, kneeling/crouching, upper-limb reach, grip/dexterity, load handling (actual load, frequency and aid), endurance, heat/cold tolerance as task-relevant.
- **Sensory:** vision (near/far/colour/depth as actually needed), hearing and alarm/communication perception, speech, tactile feedback, smell only where a validated task method genuinely relies on it (never as a substitute for gas detection).
- **Cognitive:** sustained/selective attention, alarm response, memory, sequencing, calculation, hazard recognition, decision-making, multitasking and communication.
- **Psychosocial / work pattern:** shift/night work, call-outs, isolation, time pressure, public/crew interaction, conflict exposure, responsibility and fatigue controls.
- **Emergency / critical:** evacuation, muster, first aid, rescue, firefighting, driving, control-room action or incident command only when formally assigned and trained.

For every demand, record: essential or non-essential; task(s) linked; evidence/source; frequency/duration; accommodation or alternative method considered; reviewer. Do not turn a generic job profile into a blanket medical exclusion.

## 12. Source framework for professional verification

Use the latest applicable editions and verify local legal status before operational adoption:
1. International Labour Organization (ILO), *Technical and Ethical Guidelines for Workers' Health Surveillance* (1998) – principles for health surveillance and professional ethics.
2. ILO, *Guidelines on Occupational Safety and Health Management Systems, ILO-OSH 2001* – management-system and continual-improvement framework.
3. World Health Organization (WHO), *Healthy workplaces: a model for action* (2010) – workplace health framework.
4. International Organization for Standardization, ISO 45001:2018 – OH&S management-system requirements; not a substitute for exposure limits.
5. NIOSH, *Occupational Exposure Sampling Strategy Manual* (1977) and current NIOSH sampling/analytical methods – sampling design and method selection; check method currency.
6. OSHA, 29 CFR 1910 Subpart Z and relevant standards – US regulatory examples only; not Egyptian law.
7. ACGIH, current *TLVs and BEIs* – professional guidance subject to licensing/copyright and local applicability; not automatically an Egyptian statutory limit.
8. Egyptian Labour Law and current ministerial decisions, applicable Egyptian environmental requirements and company standards – legal counsel/HSE must verify the current text, scope and applicability. Do not quote an unverified numeric limit or describe an international recommendation as Egyptian law.
9. Current Safety Data Sheets, process safety information, task risk assessments, personal/area monitoring reports, equipment manuals, permit records and emergency plans for the actual site – primary site evidence.
